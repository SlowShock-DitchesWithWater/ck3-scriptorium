**Nightmare in Britain** is an upcoming Total Conversion mod for Crusader Kings III set in the 22nd century in the British Isles, still recovering from the consequences of a world-wide nuclear conflict 100 years ago. The mod intends to radically alter the gameplay of Crusader Kings III, phasing out the world of medieval politics and intrigue in favor of a world of survival and reconstruction, while maintaining the character-focus of the game's roleplaying mechanics.

## Setting and Lore

### The Pre-War Era of *Nightmare in Britain*

**Nightmare in Britain** is set in an alternate timeline that diverged from our own in the late 19th century, where the Anglo-French rivalry endured and Britain instead aligned its interests with a liberalized Germany under Friedrich III. The Great War erupted in much the same way as our own timeline, with the exception of the United Kingdom remaining neutral in the conflict (with an assurance from Germany that Belgian neutrality would continue to be honored). Consequently, Germany emerged victorious in the war by 1918, whereas Britain remained economically stable and maintained close ties with its overseas dominions.

#### Formation of the Imperial Commonwealth

The Flag of the Imperial Commonwealth

In December of 1925, the Fifth Imperial Conference met and gathered in London to discuss the possibility of federalization. After numerous debates and compromises, the delegates from the United Kingdom and the dominions of the empire unanimously approved the Westminster Constitution on the 24th of December. On the following day, King George V delivered the "Christmas Greeting" on the radio to the British public, formally announcing the unification of the United Kingdom of Great Britain and the dominions of Canada, Ireland, Australia, New Zealand, and South Africa into the **Imperial Commonwealth.**

The formation of the Imperial Commonwealth came at a time when democratization and liberalization swept through much of Europe after Germany's victory in the Great War. By this time, the Austro-Hungarian Empire under Karl I implemented a series of reforms that restructured the country into the Danubian Federation, and the German client states of Poland, Lithuania, the United Baltic Duchy, Belarus, and Ukraine followed suit in legislating liberal policies. With the United Kingdom joining in this trend, it marked a new turning point in the dynamic of European politics.

However, such liberalization failed to materialize in the defeated countries of France and Russia, both of whom descended into civil war and required the intervention of their former German and Danubian enemies to quash all dissent. France's republican system barely managed to survive and required British and German loans to repair its national infrastructure. Russia, on the other hand, came under the rule of military leaders who led the White forces in the Russian Civil War (1917 - 1920) against the Bolshevik revolutionaries. Although it too received financial assistance, the country continued to reel from the devastation of the previous years for the next five decades.

#### A Revolution of Culture and Industry

In the time after the Great War and before the 1970s, Europe was largely at peace, and both Germany and the Imperial Commonwealth sprang forth a period of technological cooperation and advancement, most notably marked by new improvements on the *Winston Automatons* (human-like machines that were designed to simplify the working and domestic lives of the average Briton). Automatons became more prevalent after the war, with new prototypes invented to manage assembly lines, clean and care for households, and conduct deliveries on behalf of businesses. Although many among the working classes initially objected to the automation of their lines of work, government projects like the "Angel Protocol" alleviated the strains of a housing crisis and provided unemployed Britons with regular stipends to support themselves and their families.

A map of the municipal division of Greater London in the pre-war era

Beginning in the 1920s, Britain openly embraced the style and allure of the Art Deco movement, with architecture, fashion, and art adopting a blend of grandiose/traditional and modernist themes. In 1927, the Palace of Westminster was expanded to include a new tower complex that was twice the height of the Elizabeth Tower, largely based on the plans forwarded by John Pollard Seddon in 1904. Known as the "Tower of the Star," the original intent of the old plan to house imperial trophies and monuments was ignored in favor of the complex being used by representatives of the Imperial Council and the Imperial Parliament to convene and discuss matters of state. While the exterior maintained the Gothic style of the Palace of Westminster, its interior maximized the effect of Art Deco styles in corridors, offices, and even the chamber for the Imperial Parliament (known as the "Celestial Chamber" for its domed ceiling decorated with stars). As intended, the Tower of the Star served as a reminder of the Imperial Commonwealth's unity and prominence on the global stage.

Amidst this technological golden age, the boroughs of Kensington and Hammersmith were merged to form the "Imperial Collegiate," which received substantial funding from the government to expand its staff and resources for future scientific endeavors. It was also around this time when the boroughs of Erith, Bexley, and Crayford were unified under the ownership of the Barnes Company to secure a central node for further manufacturing and innovation in the field of automation.

In 1963, the British government issued the London Government Act, which reorganized the capital into the "Greater London Cosmopolis." The act established the administrative division of the capital among 39 boroughs and encouraged citizens from across the commonwealth to purchase properties and establish new businesses. By 1967, more than 600,000 citizens called Greater London their new home and over 10,000 of them founded new businesses in the form of department stores, restaurants, artisan shops, and booksellers. Consequently, the Commonwealth Chronicler declared Greater London to be "the shining sapphire star in an ocean of burgeoning promise for a great tomorrow."

**Renewed Tensions**

The Flag of the East Asian Commonwealth

Although much of the globe experienced relative peace, tensions would begin to flare up in the east. In 1942, a new militarist group known as the "Eternal Lotus" began to gain traction in China, promising to reunite the country and to restore its former glory in the face of Western and Japanese influence. Over the next seven years, popular support for the group drastically increased and other warlord factions began to waver under the growing pressure. By 1949, China was largely reunited under the Eternal Lotus, which soon rechristened the nation as the "East Asian Commonwealth."

**Work in Progress**

The British Isles, once only constituting the United Kingdom of Great Britain and the Dominion of Ireland in its time as the heartland of the Imperial Commonwealth (1925 - 2036), is now nothing more than a patchwork of states, each one struggling with the breakdown of modern society and the lingering presence of radiation from the destructive end of the Third Great War (2029 - 2036). Even as the trauma and chaos of the war continued to ravage the globe, a multitude of successor states in the British Isles managed to organize and even in some instances thrive.

### Notable Successor States

#### **The United Kingdom of Great Britain**

The Flag of the British Reconstruction and Defence Authority, most notably featuring the

broad arrow,

which was formally adopted as the symbol of the Ministry of Defence during the Third Great War

In spite of the odds, the former regional government of the United Kingdom in London had managed to survive the nuclear onslaught and reassert itself in Southern England by 2057, with both the monarchy and a majority of the British Parliament managing to reach their underground shelters in time before fallout could bear down upon the entirety of the British Isles. However, all powers of government remained in the hands of the Ministry of Defence, which assumed the mantle of administration since the initial outbreak of hostilities between the Imperial Commonwealth and the East Asian Commonwealth in 2029. In the immediate aftermath of the Third Great War, the MoD continued to exercise its authority by maintaining communications with most units of the Royal Observer Corps, dispatching soldiers (protected by gas masks and hazmat suits) to patrol barren streets and exact justice against looters, and coordinating efforts to control existing food supplies and secure what was left of the agriculture sector (which was primarily hydroponic farms in underground shelters commissioned under "Project Salvation").

A few months after the war, however, was the onset of nuclear winter, which forced the MoD to limit the flow of rations to survivors in the Salvation Shelters. Millions across Britain and Ireland had either starved or froze to death in the decade-long winter, which had finally ended by 2047. From there, the MoD acted in haste to restore the agriculture sector on the surface by enacting Contingencies Vitae-R III and Vitae-C IV, which saw surviving aircraft scatter seeds, water, and any remaining fertilizer to restore farmland across Southeastern England. Thousands of survivors emerged from their underground shelters at this time and set about rebuilding society with the assistance of MoD soldiers and other personnel. Homes and shops were rebuilt, electricity was restored, and major road networks were repaired to connect London with other southern cities that remained unscathed by the war and the winter. Surviving prototypes for solar and wind energy were replicated in a bid to compensate dwindling supplies of oil and other fossil fuels, which was then extrapolated to transportation as the surviving Barnes Company used its remaining resources to design electric vehicles.

##### **The Strethling Terror and the Wall of Westminster**

As British society continued to rebuild in the south, a new threat slowly emerged in Northern England in 2062 as feral mutants began attacking British detachments and disrupting trade between the south and the recovering north. Soon, more attacks followed and many surviving soldiers began to experience symptoms of radiation poisoning and feralization. In a matter of months, thousands of these "Strethlings" bore down upon communes of survivors across Northern England, slaughtering thousands and infecting thousands more. By the start of 2063, close to 70% of the surviving population in Northern England was either killed off or infected into Strethlings themselves, leading the MoD to recall its units from the north and begin construction on a barrier that would prevent the Strethling incursion into what was deemed the final outpost of human survivors in England. For over two decades, construction was oftentimes delayed by repeated attacks from Strethlings, which forced the MoD to actively conscript able-bodied men and women to fend off the mutants and ensure that the defence line could be completed. At last in 2086, the "Wall of Westminster" was fully constructed and manned by thousands of British soldiers. Standing 61 meters high and fitted with artillery and gunner posts, the wall was able to fully cut off any potential Strethling attack against the south, but at the cost of leaving the remaining survivors in the north to fend for themselves.

An enlistment poster by the Ministry of Defence encouraging Britons to enlist in the Wall Defence Corps

##### **The "Concession" of Power and the Situation of the United Kingdom by 2147**

After the construction of the Wall of Westminster, many across Southern England saw the work of the MoD as being complete, and that the United Kingdom was ready to see power transferred back into the hands of Parliament and the crown. In 2087, the MoD formally acquiesced and returned the powers of state to the government, but retained great sway and influence within the House of Commons and the British Armed Forces. Little of note occurred in the decades after the wall's completion, but by 2147, the situation in the south was relatively stable. Many Britons were able to enjoy their democratic freedoms again, but continued to walk about the streets of London, Southampton, and Brighton with the sense of the MoD's influence. Although the crown and the houses of Parliament were given back their powers, their reach was limited to the outskirts of Greater London. Many still wish to see the United Kingdom restored to its former borders, but the lingering Strethling threat in the north and the influence of select organizations within and beyond the country may leave such a wish as little more than a distant dream.
