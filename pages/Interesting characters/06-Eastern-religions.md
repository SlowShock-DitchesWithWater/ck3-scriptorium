# Interesting characters — Eastern religions


### Buddhist

#### Bengal

- **Narayanapala Pala, Maharaja (King) of Bengal**: Narayanapala, the seventh ruler of the esteemed Pala dynasty, presides over Bengal and Bihar during the first major decline of the empire. Under his reign, defeats at the hands of the Pratiharas led to the loss of Assam and Orissa, marking the beginning of a prolonged period of decline. This instability persisted for generations, with the empire eventually fragmenting before its revival under Mahipala I in 978. Despite the setbacks, the Pala rulers remained staunch patrons of culture and religion, establishing Hindu and Buddhist temples that enriched their realm’s legacy. Can you rewrite the fate of the Pala dynasty? Reclaim Assam and Orissa, halt the decline, and rise as a dominant force in the subcontinent. Extend your influence westward by toppling the mighty Pratihara dynasty, and forge a multi-religious Hindu-Buddhist empire that secures the Palas’ place in history as one of India’s greatest dynasties. The destiny of the Pala dynasty lays in your hands.

#### Himalaya

- **Manadeva IV Licchavi, Raja (Duke) of Nepal**: Manadeva IV, one of the last rulers of the ancient Licchavi dynasty, governs Nepal during the 9th century. His dynasty, which arrived in the region in the late 4th century, established a caste-based government and ruled the Kathmandu Valley for centuries. By his reign, however, the Licchavis were in decline, with their rule destined to end by the close of the 9th century, disappearing from history entirely. Can you defy this fate and restore the glory of the Licchavi dynasty? Develop Nepal’s infrastructure, strengthen your army, negotiate strong alliances and unite the Himalayas under your banner, forging a powerful Nepalese kingdom to dominate the region. With this newfound strength, challenge the Pala kings to the south and reclaim Bihar, the ancient homeland of your ancestors. The future of the Licchavi dynasty is in your hands—will you let it fade into obscurity, or will you rewrite its destiny forever?

#### Khorasan

- **Sūrī Muhammadzde Ghurid, Ghurshah (Duke) of Ghur**: As the last Buddhist lord west of the Indus, Sūrī stands at a crossroads — rich in silver and iron, his lands could easily become the nucleus of a powerful empire, but the hungry warlords of Khorasan to the north and Makran to the south appreciate this as well as he does. A descendent of the kings of Guzgan in days of old, Sūrī feels the weight of legacy and the pressure of current politics more keenly than most. Can you unite the quarrelsome Afghan tribes and defend your ancient spiritual heritage, or will you at last embrace the will of Allah?

#### Lanka

- **Sena Lambakanna, Maharaja (King) of Lanka**: Sena II, a ruler of the Lambakanna dynasty, is known for stabilizing the Kingdom of Lanka and leading military campaigns in southern India. Historically, he allied with Vicayâlaya Chola to defeat the Pandyas and reclaim treasures looted from Lanka during Sena I’s reign. His rule highlighted Lanka’s influence in southern India through careful consolidation and strategic involvement in regional politics. You can build on this legacy by strengthening Lanka, transforming it into a center of wealth, culture, and power. Expand by conquering Tamil lands, subjugating the Chera and Pandya kingdoms, and taking control of the Maldives. Be cautious of Vicayâlaya Chola, who could become either your strongest ally or the end of your independence. Perhaps striking first against the Chola will secure Lanka’s dominance before they rise to power. Will you forge a mighty Sinhala Empire or fall to the ambitions of the Cholas?

#### Orissa

- **Santikaradeva II Bhaumakara, Raja (Duke) of Tosali**: Santikaradeva II rules Tosali as part of the Bhaumakara dynasty, a Buddhist kingdom in the coastal region of Orissa that would later adopt Hinduism. The Bhaumakaras rose to prominence after defeating the Rashtrakuta and Pala invaders at the start of the 9th century, ending their domination over the area. This resurgence was solidified under Tribhuvana Mahadevi, Santikaradeva’s mother in-game (though historically his grandmother), following the victories of her father over the Rashtrakutas and Palas. The Bhaumakara dynasty is notable in Indian history for having five female rulers during its relatively brief reign. As Santikaradeva II, you face the looming challenges of internal strife, which historically led to the Bhaumakaras' decline in the 10th century. Can you defy destiny and prevent this collapse? Build a strong, centralized state, unify the region of Orissa, and stand firm against your powerful neighbors and former invaders—the Palas and Rashtrakutas. Will you rise to greatness, or will history take its course?

#### Guiyi

- **Luo Xing, Count of Xingqing**: Luo Xing, a vassal of Huaishen Zhang in the Guiyi Circuit, leads the Shatuo Turks—a powerful Sino-Turkic tribe that would later found dynasties like the Later Tang, Later Jin, and Later Han. A Mahayana Buddhist, Luo has the chance to overthrow his Han overlord and take control of the Guiyi Circuit, expanding his rule into eastern Tibet. Alternatively, he could embrace the Nestorian Christianity of his Öngud cousins across the border, creating a safe haven for the Church of the East in China and Tibet. For even greater ambition, Luo could lead the Shatuo south to India, founding a Nestorian Turkic dynasty and spreading his faith across the subcontinent. Whether through Buddhism or Christianity, conquest or migration, Luo Xing and the Shatuo stand ready to leave their mark on history.

#### Khotan

- **Vikarma II Vijaya, Shah (King) of Khotan**: Vikarma Vijaya, King of Khotan, rules over one of the last bastions of Mahayana Buddhism in Central Asia. As a Saka monarch of the Vijaya dynasty, he presides over a culturally rich kingdom that has long served as a bridge between Indian and Chinese civilizations. Under his reign, Khotan maintains its religious and cultural traditions amidst the shifting powers of the region. However, the rising influence of the Uyghur Khaganate to the north and the growing Islamic powers in the west pose significant threats to Khotan's autonomy and Buddhist heritage. In 867, Vikarma faces the challenge of preserving his kingdom's independence and cultural identity. Despite being one of the weaker realms in the region, it is not too late to rebuild strength, forge strong alliances, and maintain Khotan's sovereignty. Can you preserve Khotan as a stronghold of your culture and faith, resisting your Samanid, Karakhanid, Uyghur, and Tibetan neighbours, or will you be swept away by more powerful invaders? The fate of the Saka people rests on your shoulders.
- **Dharmadase Bharuka, Count of Aksu**: Dharmadase Bharuka, Count of Aksu, rules over one of the last Tocharian strongholds in the Tarim Basin, now under Uyghur domination. Once flourishing along the Silk Road, the Tocharians, an ancient Indo-European people, have seen their culture fade under centuries of conquest, from the Hephthalites to the Göktürks and now the Uyghurs of Qocho. In 867, with the Uyghur migration into the region still fresh, your position as a Buddhist ruler of a dying culture is precarious. But Aksu remains a rich oasis, and your ancestral homeland offers a glimmer of hope. Rally scattered Tocharian nobles, resist assimilation, and restore native rule to the Tarim Basin. Will you defy history and spark a Tocharian revival, or vanish into obscurity?
- **Yugaraje Jemotwona, Count of Cherchen**: Yugaraje Jemotwona, Count of Cherchen, stands as one of the last rulers of the ancient Tocharian people. As a vassal of the Saka king Vikarma II of Khotan, his holdings are populated by Mahayana Sakas, unlikely to accept the rule of a Vajrayana Tocharian like Yugaraje. With Uyghur expansion threatening from the north and Yugaraje's minority status within his liege's kingdom, his position is precarious, with little hope for survival. Yet with cunning and patience, you may build an army strong enough to overthrow your Saka overlords and reclaim Khotan, restoring Tocharian rule over the Tarim Basin. From there, you could attempt to drive out the Uyghur invaders and lead your people into a new golden age of Tocharian culture, preventing their extinction. Will you stand as a beacon for a dying people, or let them fade into history?

#### Pagan

- **Pyinbya Pagan, Duke of Pagan**: Pyinbya Pagan, founder of the city of Pagan, stands as one of the earliest historically known rulers of the Pagan Kingdom. As the great-grandfather of King Anawrahta, the future founder of the Pagan Empire, Pyinbya is remembered as a forefather of the Burmese nation. In 867, he rules amidst a fragmented Burmese landscape, offering a rare chance to unify the many peoples of the region through diplomacy, resilience, and ambition. As a follower of Ari Buddhism, a syncretic and esoteric tradition unique to Burma, Pyinbya must decide whether to maintain this local faith or embrace the growing influence of Theravada Buddhism that would later define Burmese identity. Whichever path you choose, your dynasty stands on the threshold of greatness, ready to usher in a golden age of Burmese culture and power in Southeast Asia. Will you be worthy of the legacy of the Pagan emperors and lead your people to unity and glory?

#### Gugé

- **Namdé Ösung Pugyel, Gyalpo (King) of Gugé**: Namdé Ösung Pugyel is the son of Emperor Langdarma, the last Emperor of Tibet, he rules the kingdom of Gugé in western Tibet during the turbulent Era of Fragmentation, an era caused by Emperor Langdarma’s assassination in 842, which led to a civil war between Namdé Ösung of Gugé and his brother Tridé Yumten of Ü. This war eventually caused the collapse of the once mighty Tibetan Empire, dividing it between a multitude of warlord states. As a devout follower of Tibetan Buddhism, Namdé Ösung stands in contrast to his father's Bön faith and anti-Buddhist legacy, his rule being defined by the promotion of Buddhism in western Tibet and attempts to consolidate power in the fractured realm of his father. Ultimately, Namdé Ösung was assassinated, like his father before him, cutting short his ambitions and accelerating the decline of centralised Tibetan authority, furthering the downfall of the once mighty Pugyel dynasty. In 867 however, you still have the chance to change the fate of Tibet. Can you survive the chaos of the Era of Fragmentation, defeat your traitorous brother and reunite all of Tibet under the banner of the Pugyel dynasty? Will you bring peace and prosperity to a reforged Tibetan Empire, establishing Buddhism as the unifying faith of your realm, or will the dream of a united Tibet go down in flames, destroyed by the many warlords rising up against you? The fate of Tibet rests on your shoulders.

#### China

- **Li Cui, Huángdì of Tang:** Li Cui starts the game as ruler of China during a period of tension, which is moving towards an era of division in the Dynastic Cycle. Most of his subjects have a negative opinion of him due to his sinful and unappealing personality traits. Historically, Li Cui would eventually exhaust the country’s treasury, leading to widespread rebellions that continued during his son’s reign. The Tang dynasty soon collapsed in 907, causing China to become fractured. Can you wrestle back control of the Dynastic Cycle and usher in a new period of peace for a unified China? Or will the Tang dynasty fall just like they did in history?

#### Korea

- **Sin Yeong-jae, Anchalsa of Naju:** Sin Yeong-jae is one of the few Baekje landowners in Silla, the other being Yi Geung-hyu of Jeonju. His sons are young which means you can shape them however you want. Perhaps you can try to break free from Silla and create the Kingdom of Baekje?

### Hindu

#### Andhra

- **Yuddhamala Vengi Chalukya, Raja (Duke) of Vengi**: In 867, Yuddhamala I leads the Vengi Chalukya dynasty, a long-standing power in South India that has ruled since the 7th century. The fertile and strategic region of Vengi has brought the dynasty prosperity, but powerful neighbors like the Rashtrakutas and the rising Chola dynasty pose serious threats. Historically, the Chalukyas would submit to the Cholas in the early 11th century, becoming their vassals. Despite these challenges, the Chalukyas often maintained alliances and strengthened ties through intermarriage with their neighbors. As Raja of Vengi, your task is to secure your dynasty’s future. Can you protect its independence, expand your territory, and forge your own empire in the Deccan? Or will you submit to the Rashtrakutas or Cholas temporarily, using alliances to build strength before seizing the perfect moment to overthrow your overlords? The legacy of the Vengi Chalukyas rests on your shoulders—will you forge an empire that will be remembered through the ages or let history erase your name?

#### Gondwana

- **Kokkala Cedi, Raja (Duke) of Dahala**: Raja Kokkala I rules Dahala during a time of great change in central India. His reign, lasting from around 850 to 890 CE, was marked by alliances and military campaigns that helped lay the foundation for the Cedi dynasty’s power, which would last until the 13th century. The Cedis took advantage of the declining strength of the Pratihara and Pala dynasties, expanding their lands into contested territories. During Kokkala’s time, the Cedis had a complex relationship with the Pratiharas. They may have been semi-independent vassals under Maharaja Bhoja, used their position to grow within Pratihara lands, or even subjugated parts of the Pratihara realm. These possibilities open multiple paths for your rule. You can ally with the Pratiharas, using their strength to conquer nearby vassals and challenge the Palas, eventually breaking free to secure your independence. Alternatively, you can remain independent from the start and directly challenge the Pratiharas for dominance in the region. With the Pratiharas and Palas in decline, the time is ripe for a new power to rise. Will the Cedi dynasty take its place as a dominant force in India, or will rival powers cut your ambitions short?

#### Himalaya

- **Basantadeva Katyuri, Raja (Duke) of Kurmanchal**: The Katyuri dynasty, established in the 7th century, ruled over the central Himalayas and parts of northwestern India. While some debated accounts suggest that the Katyuri Kingdom may have extended from Sikkim in the east to areas near Kabul, Afghanistan, in the west, their confirmed influence was centered in the Himalayan region. This power lasted until the 11th century when the kingdom fragmented into smaller principalities due to internal conflicts and rivalries. In 867, the Katyuri ruler is Basantadeva, known for his stable leadership and dedication to cultural and religious development, providing a strong foundation for the kingdom. As Basantadeva, you hold the opportunity to forge a powerful Himalayan Empire. Stay vigilant of the Pratiharas to the south and the Pugyel dynasty of Gugé to the north, these neighboring realms could become valuable allies in your ambitions or formidable rivals standing in the way of your destiny. Can you unite and strengthen the Katyuri Kingdom, forging an empire that stretches across the Himalayas and into Afghanistan while keeping your neighbours in check, or will you succumb to the same internal divisions that led to their decline?

#### Kabulistan

- **Kallar Shahi, Kabulistanshah (Duke) of the Shahi (Kabulistan) Kabulshahdom**: As founder of the Hindu Shahi dynasty, Kallar rose to power after deposing the last Turk Shahi ruler, inheriting an empire that traced its roots to the Hephthalites who once ruled vast swathes of Bactria and Western India. With the Saffarids pressing from the west, he sought to forge a Hindu stronghold in Afghanistan and Punjab, rallying warriors and fortifying Kabul and Ghazni as bastions of Dharma. Yet, the Hindu Shahis did not break from the past—for decades after the fall of the Turk Shahis, they maintained their administration, coinage, and institutions, reinforcing their claim as the rightful successors of the Turk Shahis. Could Kallar take this claim further? A return to the Buddhist faith of the Turk Shahis could reinforce his legitimacy and unite his diverse subjects under a new vision. By embracing this past, he could seek to reclaim Bactria in the name of the Turk Shahi legacy and then push further into India, positioning himself not merely as the last Hindu ruler of Kabul, but as the heir to the last great empire of the White Huns. Will you stand as the last shield of Hindu rule, or revive the Buddhist empire of the Turk Shahis to reclaim their homeland?

#### Kamarupa

- **Balavarman III Mlechchha, Maharaja (King) of Kamarupa**: Maharaja Balavarman III of the Mlechchha dynasty rules the kingdom of Kamarupa in 867. His dynasty, established in the mid-7th century, succeeded the Varman dynasty and governed Kamarupa until the end of the 9th century, when it was overtaken by the neighboring Pala dynasty. Balavarman III is among the last rulers of his line to enjoy a relatively stable kingdom before the Mlechchha dynasty's decline. As the only independent ruler practicing Advaita Vedanta, a unique branch of Hinduism, and belonging to the Kamrupti culture, Balavarman faces both opportunities and threats. Will you strengthen your grasp over Kamarupa, preventing the decline of your dynasty, and strike at the weakened Pala Kingdom before it recovers its strength? Or will history take its course, leading to the fall of the Mlechchha dynasty and the rise of new powers in the region?

#### Karnata

- **Avasara Silara, Mahasamanta (Duke) of Konkana**: Avasara I of the Shilahara dynasty rules over Konkana, a coastal region along the western shores of India. Originally vassals of the Rashtrakuta Empire, the Shilaharas established their rule over Konkana around the mid-8th century under their founder, Sanaphulla. Avasara I's reign, was largely uneventful, focusing on maintaining stability and governance within his domain. His successors, such as Adityavarma and Avasara II, would later engage in military and political campaigns that expanded the dynasty's influence across the region. As Avasara I, you rule during a period of peace, offering a rare opportunity to consolidate power and prepare for future expansion. Will you use this stability to fortify your realm, laying the groundwork for independence from the Rashtrakutas? Or will you rise through their ranks, securing influence from within and positioning the Shilaharas as a dominant force in the Deccan?
- **Jajjapa puri Mihirakula Huna, Samanta (Count) of Dadhipadra**: Jajjapa Huna, a Saka Shaivite noble serving under the mighty Rashtrakuta Empire, represents the remnants of the once-feared Huna people. In India, the Huna were largely descendants of the Alchon Huns (also known as the Red Huns), who once ruled much of northwestern India. Some sources also trace their lineage to the Hephthalites of Bactria (also known as the White Huns), a powerful force that invaded both India and Persia. This dual heritage places Jajjapa in a unique position, should he reclaim the legacy of the Alchons, subjugating all of Rajasthan under Hunnic rule? Or revive the Hephthalite Empire and restore Hunnic dominance over Iran? As a vassal of the Rashtrakutas, you have several choices before you. You can rise within the empire, gradually expanding into Pratihara-held Rajasthan, reclaiming the lands once ruled by your Alchon ancestors before eventually breaking free to restore Hunnic dominance over northern India. Alternatively, if you have the **Roads to Power DLC** installed, you can embrace a nomadic lifestyle and become a Landless Adventurer, abandoning your lands in southern India to embark on an ambitious journey northward. Travel back to Kabulistan, and from there, press into the Bactrian homeland of your ancestors. By adopting Afghan or Tajik culture and embracing either Buddhism or Zoroastrianism, faiths historically tied to the Hephthalites, you can unlock the decision to restore Bactrian supremacy after conquering Transoxiana, Khorasan, Kabulistan, Khuttal, and Kashgar. With the restoration of the Hephthalite Empire, you must decide: will you terrorize Persia as your ancestors once did, extending your dominion deep into Iran? Or will you turn your gaze back toward India, returning as a conqueror to subjugate the land you once left behind? Whether you choose the path of the Alchon Huns or the Hephthalites, the Huna people, who once dominated Central Asia and northern India, still have the potential to rise again as the architects of a mighty empire.

#### Kashmir

- **Avantivarman Utpala, Maharaja (King) of Kashmir**: Avantivarman, the first ruler of the Utpala kingdom of Kashmir, ascended the throne of Kashmir after the decline of the Karkota dynasty. His reign marked a period of stability and prosperity, as he focused on economic reform, cultural development, and architectural advancements. Under his rule, Kashmir flourished, with improved irrigation systems, temple construction, and a resurgence of Hindu and Buddhist patronage. However, this era of peace would not last. Following Avantivarman’s death in 883, the Utpala dynasty fell into internal strife, with his successors embroiled in succession disputes and power struggles. Despite these challenges, the dynasty remained a significant force in the region, launching successful military campaigns against the Hindu Shahi and expanding influence into Punjab. However, continued internal conflicts and factionalism weakened the dynasty, leading to its eventual collapse by the year 1003. Can you change history and bring lasting glory to the Utpala dynasty? Prevent the internal struggles that led to their downfall, consolidate Kashmir’s power, and perhaps even challenge the mighty Pratihara dynasty to establish a dominant Utpala empire in northern India. The fate of Kashmir is yours to shape.

#### Kosala

- **Bhoja Pratihara, Maharaja (King) of Kosala**: Bhoja Pratihara, one of the greatest rulers of the Pratihara dynasty and a devout follower of Vishnu, reigned in the 9th century, transforming his weakened realm into the dominant power of northern India. His empire stretched from Gujarat to Bengal and from the Narmada River to the Himalayas, engaging in prolonged conflicts with the Palas in the east and the Rashtrakutas in the south. Under his son, Mahendrapala I, the empire expanded further, reaching its territorial peak, but after his death, internal strife and succession struggles weakened the dynasty. By the early 11th century, the empire had fragmented, and in 1018 CE, Mahmud of Ghazni’s invasion of Kannauj marked the final blow to Pratihara rule. Can you rewrite history and secure the lasting dominance of the Pratihara dynasty? Consolidate your power, strengthen your realm, and prevent the internal conflicts that led to its downfall. Will you defeat the Palas and Rashtrakutas, uniting India centuries before its actual unification? The potential of the Pratiharas in the 9th century is immense, and their legacy does not have to end here. Will you rise to the challenge and lead them to eternal glory?

#### Orissa

- **Balarjuna Sivagupta Somavamsi, Raja (Duke) of Daksina Kosala**: In the 9th century, the Somavamshi dynasty emerged as a rising power in eastern India, ruling Dakshina Kosala before losing it to the expanding Cedi dynasty. Forced to abandon their homeland, they migrated eastward, overthrowing the Bhaumakara dynasty and establishing their rule along the Orissa coastline. They may have also expanded southward into Kalinga, solidifying their presence in the region. Despite being displaced, the Somavamshis never severed their ties to Dakshina Kosala, continuing to claim their ancestral lands as part of their legacy. Their reign also marked a significant cultural shift, as Buddhism waned in favor of Hinduism, shaping the religious landscape of Orissa for centuries. Can you change the fate of the Somavamshis and prevent their downfall? Secure Dakshina Kosala, crush rival powers, and forge a mighty kingdom in Orissa before your dynasty fades into obscurity. The future of the Somavamshi legacy rest upon you—will you rise to power, or will history repeat itself?
- **Anantavarman Kalinga Ganga, Raja (Duke) of Kalinga**: Anantavarman of the Kalinga Ganga dynasty ruled the coastal lands of Kalinga in the 9th century, a region that would later become a major power under Anantavarman Chodaganga in the 11th century. This great ruler would go on to expand Ganga rule into Orissa and cement the dynasty’s influence over eastern India. However, in the 9th century, little is documented about the rulers of Kalinga, as the region faced growing competition from emerging powers. The Somavamshis expanded into parts of Kalinga, challenging Ganga dominance, while the rising Chola dynasty in the south established political and marital ties with the Gangas, creating both opportunities and risks for the future of the realm. Can you steer the Kalinga Ganga through this uncertain era and establish dominance over Orissa two centuries ahead of history? Will you restore the Krishnaite faith once practiced by your ancestors and reestablish Krishna's supremacy over Kalinga? The fate of the Kalinga Ganga rests in your hands—will they rise to glory, or will they fade into obscurity as history marches on?

#### Punjab

- **Abhay Chandra Katoch, Thakur (Count) of Trigarta**: In the 9th century, Trigarta was ruled by the Katoch dynasty, one of the oldest royal lineages in Indian history, tracing its origins to Rajanaka Bhumi Chand. Known for their martial prowess and military cunning, the Katoch rulers once commanded great influence, but by 867, their power had waned. Abhay Chandra Katoch finds himself ruling a vulnerable realm, surrounded by far stronger polities such as the mighty Pratiharas, the ambitious Utpalas of Kashmir, and the powerful Kingdom of Gugé in the Himalayas. To survive and rise, you must navigate this dangerous political landscape. Becoming a vassal of the Pratiharas could offer protection while you consolidate power and prepare for future expansion. With patience and strategy, you may one day lay claim to all of Punjab and forge an independent Katoch kingdom, restoring the dynasty’s former glory. Will you prove yourself worthy of your legendary ancestors, or will you fade into obscurity, another forgotten relic of history?

#### Tamilakam

- **Vicayâlaya Chola, Raja (Duke) of Chola Nadu**: Vicayâlaya Chola, a mighty warlord of southern India, once served as a vassal of the Pallava dynasty of Pakanadu. During a conflict between the Pallavas and the Pandyas, he seized his opportunity—breaking free from Pallava rule and capturing Tanjavur. Despite this initial success, the Chola realm would soon face setbacks: Vicayâlaya’s advancing age and weakened position allowed the Pandyas to reassert dominance, forcing him to pay tribute. True independence and lasting dominance over the region would only be secured by his descendants. In 867, history is not yet written. As Vicayâlaya Chola, you are a mighty conqueror, but surrounded by enemies on all sides. With cunning diplomacy and shrewd alliances, you can defeat the treacherous Pallavas and Pandyas and establish a glorious Chola Empire in southern India generations ahead of time. Will you change the fate of this great conqueror and carve a new destiny for the Cholas, or will history repeat itself and see your ambitions crushed?

### Jain

#### Maharastra

- **Amoghavarsha Rashtrakuta, Maharaja (King) of the Rashtrakuta (Maharastra) Kingdom**: The 6th and greatest ruler of the Rashtrakuta dynasty, a person who devoted himself to the pacifistic nature of his religion. Already an old man, will you continue his legacy as his successors of a cultural powerhouse expanding only through marriage, or will you form a lasting Deccan Empire across all of India by the sword?

### Taoist

Now, he has abdicated and left the command in your hands. Starting with a strong and wealthy realm, you have options for expansion and development, but beware of the nomadic tribes in the north. Still, with enough ambition you can restore entire Tibet under your control, expanding the reach of China ever onwards.

### Confucian

#### China

- **Huáng Chāo:** Chāo has struggled to pass exams and obtain the status of civil servant. Born into a family of salt smugglers, he gained boldness and wealth through a life outside the law, eventually leading a rebellion aptly named after him. Chāo managed to capture the Tang capital of Chang’an and declared himself Emperor but was supposedly killed four years later by his own nephew. Soon after starting the game, a unique event appears that determines your future direction. It is up to you which path you take. Will you rewrite history and become a respected Minister, or will you attempt to rise up against the Huángdì?
