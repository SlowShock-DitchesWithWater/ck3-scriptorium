# Interesting characters — Muslim characters


### Ash'ari

#### Africa

- **Muhammad II Aghlabid, Sultan (King) of Africa**: In 867, Sultan Muhammad II begins his reign embroiled in war with the Byzantine Empire over control of Sicily, a conquest that would only be completed under his zealous and ruthless brother, Sultan Ibrahim II. Muhammad, known for his preference for leisure like hunting and drinking over ambition, presided over a period of stability that prepared the Aghlabid realm for future expansions. Upon his death in 875, Ibrahim II ascends as a fanatical ruler, enforcing Sunni law with an iron fist and earning a reputation for both military brilliance and madness. Ibrahim went on to continue the conquest of southern Italy and lead daring raids against the Byzantines. Dreams of dominating the Mediterranean, with whispers of an audacious plan to march on Constantinople, can you fulfil the contrasting legacies of these brothers, stabilizing the realm under Muhammad and creating a Mediterranean empire under Ibrahim, or will internal unrest and the looming Karling-Byzantine alliance spell the end of the Aghlabids?

#### Al-Andalus

- **Adanis ibn Awsaga Banu Danis, Emir (Duke) of Baya (Beja)**: Tasked by the Umayyad sultan with protecting the coast from Viking raids, the Berber Banu Danis dynasty has quickly grown in influence and power. Emir Adanis has built strong fortresses in Alcácer and Coimbra, but is concerned about the Mozarabs under his rule, who would rather side with his nemesis, Ibn Marwan. Can you secure your lands and lead the Berbers to a great future in Al-Andalus?

#### Anbiya

- **al-Athîr ibn Bâtin Lamtuna, Emir (Duke) of Adrar**: In 867, al-Athîr rules the Lamtuna tribe in the Saharan region, the same tribe from which the Almoravid dynasty would later emerge. He’s well-positioned to unite the Berber tribes of the area and form the Kingdom of Anbiya. From there, you can follow history by invading Morocco to expand your power base, then establish a new branch of your dynasty to emulate the transition between the Lamtuna and the Almoravids. Once established, you can push into Andalusian lands to strengthen Islamic rule and secure dominion over Iberia. Additionally, if you’re feeling bold, march south into Ghana to take control of their gold mines and dominate the gold and slave trade of West Africa.

#### Arabian Empire

- **Al-Mu'tazz ibn Al-Mutawakkil Abbasid, Caliph (Emperor) of the Abbasid (Arabian) Empire**: Theoretically, Al-Mu'tazz should be the most powerful man in the Islamic world. But the youngest caliph since the establishment of the institution is an indulgent wastrel: callous, cynical, arbitrary, greedy, and lustful. He is beset by powerful vassals, ready to rebel, including his half-brothers Al-Muwaffak and Al-Mu'tamid. Historically, he was one of the caliphs who ruled during the Anarchy at Samarra. His reign (and the reigns of his two predecessors) were marked by a tremendous loss of power by the caliphs to regional emirs. Can you turn around the historical decline of the caliphate? Alternatively, you can play as one of Al-Mu'tazz's arguably more deserving brothers, Al-Muwaffak or Al-Mu'tamid, and try to overthrow your corrupt ruler and claim the caliphate for yourself. Note: May not be as easy as it looks.
- **Muhammad Armani, Emir (Duke) of Cilicia**: An Armenian military commander serving the Abbasid Caliphate, Muhammad was historically appointed governor of Cilicia in 872, ruling briefly before his death in 873. However, in the game, his story begins earlier, in 867, presenting the opportunity to rewrite history. Starting with a domain ranging from Tarsus to Antioch, Muhammad’s lands are predominantly populated by Christians, he will have to choose between tolerance, zealous conversion or maybe considering a new faith for himself. As volatile as it may be, Muhammad can use this situation to launch bold campaigns into neighbouring territories, such as the rich lands of Anatolia or the Armenian region of Edessa. Through decisive victories and relentless ambition, he could carve out a powerful domain on the Byzantine frontier, perhaps even forging an independent empire, a Greco-Armenian Islamic state rising where Byzantium once stood.

#### Daylam

- **Rostam Karenzade Bavandid, Vālī (Count) of Mazandaran**: Rostam's family has a pedigree almost unmatched in the known world: his father's line are some of the last of the Sassanids, whilst his wife traces her descent back to the near-millennium old Arsacids of Armenia. Old dynasties and ancient faiths endure well in mountainous Tabaristan, where Daylamite mountain lords cling to generations-long grudges and many, Rostam amongst them, still grapple with the "new" dominance of Islam over the traditions of yesteryear. Can you reclaim the venerable glories of your birthright, or will you embrace the modern world?

#### Khorasan

- **Muhammad Tahirzde Tahirid, Amir-e Amiran (King) of Khorasan**: As the last great ruler of the Tahirids, Muhammad ibn Tahir faces a dire threat in 867 from Ya'qub Laythzade, the Saffarid warlord whose meteoric rise threatens to wrest Khorasan from his grasp. Historically, the loss of Khorasan marked the end of Tahirid power, but this doesn’t have to be Muhammad’s fate. By repelling the Saffarid invaders, he could secure his position and turn his sights on the weakening Abbasid Caliphate, which once tried to deny him his birthright—the rule of Khorasan. Perhaps it is time to show the Caliph that his authority no longer holds. Embracing Shia Islam, Muhammad could rally the restless Persian populace and march on Baghdad, proclaiming himself Emperor of a reborn Persia. A new empire could rise, rivaling the Abbasids at their zenith, but why stop there? Extend your influence over all Arabia and prove to the invaders that Persia is not theirs to command.

#### Makran

- **Ya'qub ibn al-Layth, Amir-e Amiran (King) of Makran**: The son of a lowly coppersmith, Ya'qub is a self-made man whose disfigurement by a sword stroke has neither slowed nor hindered him. Having carved out a large kingdom in Sistan, he is now embroiled in war with the Tahirids, the caliph's old lackeys. On the home front, the same commanders that supported Ya'qub's usurpation whisper of changing their loyalties yet again. You seem poised to forge a new destiny for Persia — do you dare challenge the enduring might of the Abbasids?
- **Salih kurê al-Hujr Zunbil, Vālī (Count) of Zamindawar**: Salih Zunbil, last scion of the Hephthalite Zunbil dynasty, rules Zamindawar as a Saffarid vassal, outwardly Muslim but secretly devoted to Zun, the Sun-God of Zabulistan. His ancestors were once part of the mighty Hephtalite Empire, where Zoroastrian, Buddhist, Christian, and Manichaean traditions thrived, but now his people languish under foreign rule. Breaking free alone is difficult, but by embracing Buddhism, still strong in Afghanistan, he could rally eastern allies and reclaim Sistan, Zabulistan, and Kabul while secretly preserving Zunism. Once his rule extends across Zabulistan, Khorasan and Transoxiana, he may restore the Bactrian Empire and reveal his true faith, establish its priesthood, and make Zun the supreme deity of a reborn Hephthalite empire. Will you balance faith and deception to restore the glory of Zun, or will your lineage fade away, it's ancient history forever forgotten?

#### Persia

- **Muhammad ibn Abdallah, Amir (Duke) of Fars**: A professional administrator, Muhammad has served the Abbasids faithfully throughout his long career as governor of Iraq, the Holy Cities, and even Baghdad itself. Still, the recent turmoil has taxed even his political acumen, as he narrowly escaped death after siding with the wrong caliph. Even now, his health begins to wane. Will you spend your twilight years supporting your beleaguered nephew, the Amir of Khorasan, or focus on redeeming your family in the eyes of the caliph?

#### Punjab

- **Muhammad Banu Sama, Amir (Duke) of Multan**: Amir Muhammad of the Arabic Banu Sama dynasty, rules the wealthy and strategic emirate of Multan, a crossroads of trade and culture in the Punjab region. Known as the "Frontier House of Gold" for its prosperity and the revered Sun Temple, Multan thrives amid a diverse population of Muslims and Hindus. Surrounded by Hindu rulers in Punjab and an Islamic sultanate in Sindh, you can expand your domain through decisive conquest while respecting and integrating Hindu traditions and communities into your growing realm. Perhaps you could even create your own local sect of Islam, incorporating Eastern syncretism into the core tenets of your beliefs. With Multan as a model of coexistence under strong leadership, push further into India, forging a powerful, multi-religious state that unites the wealth and culture of the subcontinent under your banner.

#### Sicily

- **Sawdan Sawdanid, Emir (Duke) of the Sawdanid (Apulia) Emirate**: Responsible for the pillaging of Farfa and many other bountiful raids, at the turning point of his political career, Sawdan seems to have run out of luck. Stuck in Southern Italy with the scheming Byzantines to his east and the Karling hordes rallying to his capital, it seems like the dream of a great Italian sultanate, after barely a generation, has run its course. Will you turn the tide and defeat the Karlings, or will the Emirate of Bari find itself between a Karling and a hard place for the last time?

#### Sindh

- **Umar ibn'Abd al-Aziz al'Habbari, Sultan (King) of Sindh**: Umar rules the independent Habbari Sultanate from Mansura, a prosperous hub on the Indus River. Historically, the Habbari dynasty broke away from Abbasid control in 861, establishing a fully independent state while maintaining trade and cultural ties to the broader Islamic world. Sindh’s large Isma’ili population offers a unique opportunity—embrace their faith to unite the realm under Shia Islam or maintain Sunni orthodoxy to ensure stability. Free from Abbasid oversight, Umar can expand his influence into Multan, Makran, or Gujarat, transforming Sindh into a dominant power in South Asia. The future of Sindh lies in your hands.

#### Transoxiana

- **Eraq Mansurzk Afrighid, Khwarezmshah (Duke) of Khwarezm**: Eraq Mansurzk rules Khwarezm, a vital region along the Amu Darya River. The Afrighids, rulers since the 4th century, converted to Islam to align with Abbasid authority, though Zoroastrian traditions still linger among the populace. Eraq faces growing pressure from the rising Samanid dynasty, which historically reduced the Afrighids to vassals by the early 10th century. Amidst Abbasid decline, Eraq could pursue an alternate path, rekindling Zoroastrianism to rally his people against Samanid encroachment. Such a bold move could alienate potential Islamic allies but establish Khwarezm as a bastion of independence and cultural revival. By rallying the Zoroastrian population within the Samanid realm, Eraq might even dominate the region as the ruler of a resurgent Khurmazta Kingdom. Alternatively, embracing Islam fully could unify his realm under a single faith, secure powerful alliances, and position Khwarezm to rival the Samanids as the dominant power in Transoxiana. The fate of Khwarezm rests on your decisions.
- **Muhtaj zk Masud Chaghanid, Vālī (Count) of Chaghaniyan**: Muhtaj Chaghanid is a member of the Chaghanid (or Muhtajid) dynasty, likely descended from the ancient Chaghan Khudas who once ruled the region under the Hephtalite Empire. By 867, your family is a minor power in the principality of Chaghaniyan, serving under the rising Samanid dynasty. Embrace your ancient legacy, reclaim the glories of your Hephtalite ancestors, and seize the opportunity to defy your weakened overlords. Renounce Islam and the Caliph’s influence over Persia in favor of the ancestral faiths of your people, such as Khurmazta Zoroastrianism or Buddhism. From there, re-establish Bactrian influence across Khorasan and Transoxiana, reviving the fear and power the Hephtalites once commanded centuries earlier. Will you restore the glory of your lineage, or fade into the footnotes of someone else’s history?

#### Yemen

- **Aarif Dofharid, Chieftain of Dhufar**: You start as an independent tribal chieftain on the shore of the Indian Ocean, and your neighbors, the Ibadi tribe of Mahra, are ripe for conquest. That’s enough to form the Duchy of Mahra and put you on a path to become the Sultan of Yemen, but beware: you’ll have to contend with the Abbasids, the Abyssinians, and potentially even the Pratiharans.

### Ibadi

#### Sahara

- **al-Khattab Banu Khattab, Sheikh (Count) of Ghat**: al-Khattab Banu Khattab rules the oasis town of Ghat in the Fezzan region, a crucial hub along the trans-Saharan trade routes. Historically, the area was home to numerous Berber tribes, fiercely independent and masters of desert warfare. The Banu Khattab dynasty, followers of the Ibadi sect of Islam, embodied a distinct identity in a Sunni-dominated North Africa, often resisting outside control. al-Khattab could rally the Butr and Zaghawa peoples of the region, uniting them to form the Kingdom of Sahara as a bastion of Ibadi Islam. From this stronghold, he could raid the prosperous Aghlabids in Ifriqiya or the Tulunids of Egypt, using their wealth to expand his influence and eventually take over their lands. With daring ambition, he might even march on Mecca and Medina, reclaiming them for the Ibadi faithful and challenging Sunni dominance across the Islamic world. The future of the Banu Khattab dynasty and their legacy in the desert rests in your hands.

#### Tahert

- **Aflah ibn Wahab Rustamid, Emir (Duke) of the Rustamid (Alger) Emirate**: You are the Ibadi emir, an adherent of a Muslim sect that predates the Islamic schism, and you represent the group of them that fled to North Africa. Your neighbors are the Ash'ari Aghlabids to the east, the Zayidi Idrisids to the west, and to the north just across the sea, the Muwalladi Umayyads and the Catholic Karlings. All of the above are more powerful than you, and they can all declare Holy War on you... Can you claim Mecca and Medina for your creed?

### Maturidi

#### Transoxiana

- **Isma'il Ahmadzde Samanid, Vālī (Count) of Bukhara**: For decades, Ismail's family have dominated the governorships of Transoxiana. The sundering of the Caliphate shattered that central authority, leaving the Samanids to squabble amongst themselves for rule. Young Ismail's chance to vie for power comes as he is welcomed as ruler of Bukhara, recently sacked by the armies of the fierce western Khwarezmshahs. Despite his prodigious talents, the rest of his family expect complete, loyal submission. Can you surpass your brothers and restore the glory of Persia?
- **Nasr Ahmadzde Samanid, Amir-e Amiran (King) of Transoxiana**: Nasr Samanid, ruler of Transoxiana, stands at the dawn of the Samanid dynasty’s rise to power. While officially under Abbasid authority, Nasr holds autonomous control over much of Central Asia. While surrounded by many rivals such as the Tahirids, Khwarezmian Afrighids and rising Saffarids, the true threat to his rule lies within his realm, in the form of his ambitious younger brother Isma'il, governor of Bukhara, who would eventually overthrow him. In 867 however, there is still time to change Nasr's fate, strenghten your position in Transoxiana, keep your brother loyal, or make him disappear permanently and extent your influence further into Persia by subjugating the Afrighids and invading the weakening Tahirids. With enough dedication you can lay the foundations for a powerful and stable Samanid Empire of Persia. Will you preserve the unity of the Samanid dynasty, securing their grasp over Persia, or will you let your dynasty be torn apart from within?
- **Ibrahim zk Parsak Bukhara Khuda, Vālī (Count) of Nakhshab**: Ibrahim Bukhara Khuda is one the last scions of the ancient Bukhara Khudas, Sogdian rulers who once held the proud city of Bukhara. Now reduced to a minor count under the rising Samanid dynasty, Ibrahim rules from Nakhshab, having converted to Maturidi Islam, despite this many of his subjects still follow the old Zoroastrian faith of Khurmazdism. Given this predicament, you now stand at a crossroads. Will you return to your ancestral Zoroastrian religion, lead a revolt, and reclaim Bukhara to restore Sogdian rule over Transoxiana? Or will you embrace your new Islamic identity and seek allies to overthrow your Samanid overlords and establish a new Caliphate in northern Persia? Will you be worthy of the rich legacy of the Bukhara Khudas?

### Mu'tazili

#### Daylam

- **Baduspan Karen, Vālī (Count) of Dihistan**: Your family claims lineage dating back to the Parthian Empire of old, but those glory days are long gone. Though outwardly following Islam, you still hold sympathy to the Khurramite movement that rebelled against the Caliphate. Will you stay on the offensive and become the one who brings benefit, or will you fully embrace Allah?

### Muwalladi

#### Al-Andalus

- **Abd al-Rahman ibn Marwan al-Yiliqi, Emir (Duke) of Batalyaws (Badajoz)**: A rogue at heart, Abd al-Rahman ibn Marwan has a tumultuous relationship with his liege, the Umayyad sultan. But he enjoys strong support from the Mozarabs in his domain, and might in truth have more in common with them and their faith. He has carved out a stronghold in Badajoz, but in the west, the zealous Banu Danis clan grows ever more opposed to his rule over the Lower March. Might an alliance with Alfonsu III, Asturias' ever-opportunistic king, help you gain a crown of your own?
- **Lubb Muzez Banu Musa, Sheikh (Count) of Najera**: The powerful dynasty of the Banu Qasi, the Sons of Cassius, once overlooked all the Ebro Valley. Hard times have befallen them since the reign of Lubb's glorious father, though, and the newly-renamed Banu Musa merely mutter and lick their wounds from their ancient seat at Tudela, plotting to recover their southern dominions. Will you be able to retake what once belonged to the Banu Qasi and bring the dynasty back to its former glory?
- **Ya'far Hafsunid, Sheikh (Count) of Malaqah**: Ya'far Hafsunid, Sheikh of Malaqah, is the ancestor of a dynasty that would one day shake the foundations of Al-Andalus. His grandson, Umar ibn Hafsun, would go on to lead a major revolt against the Umayyad Emirate, rallying the Muwallads and Mozarabs of the south. In a bold political move, Umar eventually converted to Christianity, seeking support from Alfonso III of Asturias and the mighty Ibn Marwan. Though his rebellion ultimately failed with his death in 917, history does not have to follow the same path. As Ya'far, you can change the future—lay the groundwork for a stronger revolt, and once your heir Umar rises to power, unite the discontented peoples of Al-Andalus, convert to Mozarabism, and forge a lasting Christian Andalusian state in the heart of Iberia. Will the Hafsunids rise to glory and reshape the fate of Spain, or vanish into the shadows like so many rebels before them?

#### Krete

- **Shu'ayb ibn Umar Hafsid, Emir (Duke) of Krete**: Shu'ayb ibn Umar Hafsid rules the Emirate of Krete from the fortified city of Chandax. Founded by Andalusian exiles, Krete prospers as a hub of trade and naval raids, yet it faces relentless pressure from the Byzantine Empire, determined to reclaim the island. Historically, the emirate fell to Byzantine forces in 961, just a century later, making its survival a formidable challenge. Shu'ayb could rewrite history by harnessing Krete’s naval strength and forging alliances, such as with the nearby Abbasids, to expand into the Aegean Islands, Hellas, and Cyprus, steadily undermining Byzantine control. With these territories secured, he could establish a foothold on the Greek mainland and, with enough ambition, dismantle the Byzantine Empire to create a Greco-Andalusian realm in its place. The task is daunting, but with strong leadership, Crete could dominate the eastern Mediterranean and leave an enduring legacy. For the truly bold, there’s the challenge of the “Iberia or Iberia?” achievement, which requires controlling the Caucasian Iberia region as a character of Iberian heritage. This would mean expanding far into Georgia and Armenia. Will you rise to greatness, or will Krete fall to Byzantine conquest?

### Zayidi

#### Daylam

- **Wahsudan Justanid, Vālī (Count) of Gilan**: Starts right next to the Abbasids, so survival will be a struggle. Still, the weak Alavids are right next door, and you can always try to swear fealty to the caliphate and destroy it from within. According to legend, it was Wahsudan who built the future Hashshāshīn stronghold of Alamut.

#### Maghreb

- **Yahya II ibn Yahya Idrisid, Sultan (King) of Maghreb**: Yahya II ibn Yahya rules the Idrisid Sultanate in the Maghreb, a Zayidi Shia stronghold in a region dominated by Sunni Islam. Historically, he was a weak ruler who divided his realm among relatives and distanced himself from politics, leading to a relatively stable reign until his indulgent lifestyle forced him to flee his palace and die shortly after in Fes. However, under your guidance, this decadent ruler’s fate could change. Strengthen your kingdom, subdue your Sunni, Sufri, Ibadi, and Najdat rivals, and expand your influence southward to claim the rich salt mines of Ijil and Taghaza. With enough ambition, turn your gaze northward to Iberia—overthrow the weakened Umayyads and seize Al-Andalus for yourself, ushering in a new golden age for the Baranis people and the Shia faith.

### Isma'ili

- **Aarif ibn Bahram Jannabid, Chieftain (Count) of Al-Hasa**: Aarif ibn Bahram Jannabid represents the Qarmatian Jannabid dynasty, a family of Persian origin that would later found the radical Qarmatian state. His father, Bahram Jannabid, rules lands in Bahrain. Still a follower of mainstream Isma'ili Islam, Aarif must embrace Qarmatianism, a revolutionary sect that would one day terrorise the Abbasid Caliphate and the Islamic world for more than a century. Historically, under the leadership of Aarif’s son, the warlord Abu Tahir al-Jannabi, the Qarmatians would raid the holy city of Mecca, burn the Kaaba, slaughter countless pilgrims, defile the Zamzam well with their bodies, and steal the Black Stone, only returning it twenty years later, shattered into seven pieces. These acts were committed in order to break with the Islamic world and in the belief that they would hasten the coming of the Mahdi to usher in the end of days. The Qarmatian state also abolished Sharia law and adopted many Zoroastrian practices, creating a society that shocked and terrified the Islamic world. As Aarif, you can follow in the footsteps of your historical counterparts: unite Bahrain and Al-Hasa under your rule through inheritance or conquest, seize the strategic city of Basra, and break free from Abbasid control. From there, expand into Oman and build a powerful Qarmatian kingdom along the Arabian coast. Use your new position to raise a mighty force of Qarmatian zealots, plunder Mecca and other holy cities, and spread terror and death throughout the Islamic world. Perhaps, you can even succeed where the Qarmatians failed and establish Qarmatian supremacy across Arabia and beyond. The legacy of the Qarmatians is written in terror, blood, and zealotry. In the words of Abu Tahir al-Jannabi: "I am by God, and by God I am... He creates creation, and I destroy them."

### Azariqa

- **Musawir kurê Abd al-Ḥamīd al-Bawazij, Vālī (Count) of Hadithat-Ana**: Musawir al-Bawazij, a formidable Kurdish warlord, leads the Kharijite Azariqa rebellion against the mighty Abbasid Caliphate. Known for their radical beliefs and fierce opposition to the established Islamic authority, the Azariqa considered all Muslims outside their movement to be apostates, unbelievers, and hypocrites, sanctioning their systematic execution without exception, even for women and children. Strikingly, this brutal standard did not apply to the Dhimmis (Christians and Jews) of the region, who were seen as faithfully following their own prophets. Unlike the broader Islamic world, the Azariqa preached equality between men and women, drawing inspiration from the women who fought alongside the Prophet Muhammad, and rejected all ethnic hierarchies within Islam. Historically, the Kharijite rebellion would end in defeat, with the Abbasids crushing the Azariqa movement. But in 867, the rebellion is in full swing, and its outcome is still undecided. Take up arms against the oppression of the Caliph and lead the Azariqa to victory, forging your own independent polity in the heart of Jazira. From there, spread terror among your enemies, shatter the Abbasid Caliphate, and turn Azariqa into the dominant branch of Islam. Through zeal, blood, and the might of jihad, your people can rise and reign supreme. But beware, the might of the Caliph remains great, and if you fail, your radical dream may be crushed and forgotten under the heel of Abbasid vengeance.
